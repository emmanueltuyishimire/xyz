<h2>When Does the 2026 COLA Take Effect?</h2>

<p>
  The 2026 Social Security COLA took effect as follows:
</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Social Security retirement, disability (SSDI), and survivor benefits:</strong> The increase applied starting with the December 2025 benefit, which was deposited in <strong>January 2026</strong>.</li>
  <li><strong>Supplemental Security Income (SSI):</strong> Increased SSI payments began on <strong>December 31, 2025</strong> — the last business day of the month — to reflect the January 2026 effective date.</li>
</ul>
<p>
  You do not need to apply for the COLA increase. SSA applies it automatically. In December 2025, SSA
  mailed personalized COLA notices to all beneficiaries showing the new gross benefit amount,
  Medicare Part B deduction, and net monthly payment. You can also view your updated figures at
  <a href="https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ssa.gov/myaccount ↗</a>.
</p>

<h2>How Is the Social Security COLA Calculated?</h2>

<p>
  The annual COLA is not a political decision or a congressional vote. It is an automatic calculation
  established under the Social Security Amendments of 1972, tied to the
  <strong>Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W)</strong>,
  published monthly by the Bureau of Labor Statistics.
</p>
<p>
  The SSA compares the average CPI-W index value for <strong>July, August, and September</strong>
  of the current year to the average CPI-W for the same three months of the prior year (or the last
  year a positive COLA was approved). The percentage change between those two averages becomes the
  next year's COLA rate.
</p>
<p>
  For the 2026 COLA: the third-quarter 2025 CPI-W average was compared to the third-quarter 2024
  average. The result was a 2.8% increase, which SSA announced on October 24, 2025 after the
  September 2025 CPI-W data was released by BLS.
</p>
<p>
  If the CPI-W shows zero or negative change, the COLA is set at 0%. Social Security benefits
  are never cut due to deflation.
</p>

<h2>Does the 2026 COLA Affect Medicare Costs?</h2>

<p>
  The Social Security COLA does not determine Medicare Part B premiums — CMS sets them independently
  each year. In 2026, the standard monthly Part B premium rose to <strong>$202.90</strong> (up $17.90
  from $185.00 in 2025). The annual Part B deductible increased to <strong>$283</strong> in 2026.
</p>
<p>
  Because most Medicare-enrolled Social Security recipients have their Part B premium automatically
  deducted from their check, this $17.90 monthly increase directly reduces the net impact of the
  2026 COLA increase on what you actually deposit.
</p>

<h3>The Hold Harmless Rule: What It Means for You</h3>
<p>
  Federal law — specifically Section 1839(f) of the Social Security Act — includes the
  <strong>Hold Harmless rule</strong>. It ensures that if the Medicare Part B premium increase
  would reduce your net Social Security check below the prior year's level, the premium is
  capped so your check does not shrink.
</p>
<p>
  In 2026, the Part B increase of $17.90 did not exceed the dollar value of the 2.8% COLA
  for most beneficiaries, so the Hold Harmless rule was not widely triggered. However, for
  beneficiaries with very low monthly benefits, the $17.90 Part B increase could exceed their
  COLA dollar increase — in those cases, Hold Harmless protects the net check.
</p>
<p>
  Hold Harmless <strong>does not apply</strong> if you: are newly enrolled in Medicare Part B,
  pay Part B by quarterly bill rather than Social Security deduction, or are subject to IRMAA
  income-related premium surcharges.
</p>

<h2>Does the 2026 COLA Affect Taxes?</h2>

<p>
  A higher Social Security benefit from the 2026 COLA can affect how much of your benefits are
  subject to federal income tax — depending on your total income.
</p>
<p>
  The IRS uses a measure called <strong>Provisional Income</strong> (also called Combined Income):
  your adjusted gross income + non-taxable interest + 50% of your total Social Security benefit.
  The federal thresholds are fixed by law and do not adjust for inflation:
</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Single filers:</strong> If Provisional Income exceeds $25,000, up to 50% of your Social Security may be taxable. Above $34,000, up to 85% may be taxable.</li>
  <li><strong>Married filing jointly:</strong> Thresholds are $32,000 and $44,000.</li>
</ul>
<p>
  Because these thresholds never adjust for inflation, a COLA increase can push some beneficiaries
  across a threshold for the first time. We recommend using our
  <a href="/tools/retirement-tax-calculator/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Retirement Tax Estimator ↗</a>
  after any COLA increase to recalculate your tax exposure.
</p>
<p>
  To adjust federal withholding from your Social Security check, file
  <strong>Form W-4V</strong> (Voluntary Withholding Request) with the SSA. You can choose 7%, 10%,
  12%, or 22% withholding.
</p>

<h2>What About SSI, SSDI, and Other Benefits?</h2>

<p>
  The 2026 COLA of 2.8% applied to all Social Security programs, including:
</p>
<ul>
  <li>
    <strong>Supplemental Security Income (SSI):</strong> The federal SSI benefit increased by 2.8% in 2026.
    The maximum federal SSI payment for an individual rose to <strong>$994/month</strong>; for couples,
    to <strong>$1,491/month</strong>. SSI payments increased on <strong>December 31, 2025</strong>
    (reflecting the January 2026 effective date).
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI):</strong> SSDI benefits received the same 2.8% COLA
    increase as retirement and survivor benefits, effective January 2026.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Veterans' benefits (VA):</strong> VA disability compensation and pension programs are adjusted
    by the same COLA rate as Social Security, under a separate federal provision. The 2.8% rate also
    applied to VA benefits beginning December 1, 2025 (paid January 1, 2026).
  </li>
</ul>
<p>
  The maximum taxable earnings base for workers also adjusted in 2026: wages up to <strong>$184,500</strong>
  are subject to Social Security payroll taxes, up from $176,100 in 2025.
</p>

    <li>Contact your SHIP counselor at <a href="https://www.shiphelp.org" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer" style="color: #C9933A;">shiphelp.org ↗</a> or call 1-877-839-2675 if you believe your Hold Harmless protection was not applied correctly.</li>
  </ol>
