<h2>When Does the 2027 COLA Take Effect?</h2>

<p>
  The 2027 Social Security COLA takes effect as follows:
</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Social Security retirement, disability (SSDI), and survivor benefits:</strong> The increase applies starting with the December 2026 benefit, which is deposited in <strong>January 2027</strong>. Payment dates depend on your birth date (second, third, or fourth Wednesday of the month).</li>
  <li><strong>Supplemental Security Income (SSI):</strong> Increased SSI payments begin on <strong>December 31, 2026</strong> — the last business day before January 1, 2027 — because January 1 is a federal holiday and SSI payments cannot be delayed.</li>
</ul>
<p>
  You do not need to apply for the COLA increase. SSA applies it automatically to your record. Starting
  in late November 2026, SSA will send personalized COLA notices showing your new gross benefit,
  Medicare Part B deduction, and net monthly payment. You can also view your updated figures at
  <a href="https://www.ssa.gov/myaccount" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">ssa.gov/myaccount ↗</a>.
</p>

<h2>How Is the Social Security COLA Calculated?</h2>

<p>
  The annual COLA is an automatic calculation — not a political decision or a congressional vote.
  It is established under the Social Security Amendments of 1972 and codified at
  <strong>42 U.S.C. § 415(i)</strong>, tied to the
  <strong>Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners and Clerical Workers (CPI-W)</strong>,
  published monthly by the Bureau of Labor Statistics.
</p>
<p>
  The SSA compares the average CPI-W index value for <strong>July, August, and September</strong>
  of the current year to the average CPI-W for the same three months of the prior year (or the last
  year a positive COLA was approved). The percentage change between those two averages becomes the
  next year's COLA rate.
</p>
<p>
  For the 2027 COLA: the third-quarter 2026 CPI-W average was higher than the third-quarter 2025
  average by 3.6%, reflecting sustained consumer price inflation during mid-2026. The BLS released
  the final September 2026 CPI-W reading on October 14, 2026, and SSA announced the COLA the same morning.
</p>
<p>
  If the CPI-W shows zero or negative change, the COLA is set at 0%. Social Security benefits
  are never cut due to deflation.
</p>

<h2>What Most Summaries Leave Out: The Net Check vs. the Gross COLA</h2>

  from their check each month. When CMS raises the Part B premium (announced separately in November),
  that increase reduces the net deposit.
</p>

<h3>The Hold Harmless Rule</h3>
<p>
  Federal law — Section 1839(f) of the Social Security Act — includes the
  <strong>Hold Harmless rule</strong>. It ensures that if the Medicare Part B premium increase
  would reduce your net Social Security check below the prior year's level, the premium is
  capped so your net check does not shrink.
</p>
<p>
  Hold Harmless <strong>does not apply</strong> if you: are newly enrolled in Medicare Part B,
  pay Part B by quarterly bill rather than Social Security deduction, or are subject to IRMAA
  income-related premium surcharges.
</p>

<h2>Does the 2027 COLA Affect Taxes?</h2>

<p>
  A higher Social Security benefit from the 2027 COLA can affect how much of your benefits are
  subject to federal income tax — depending on your total income.
</p>
<p>
  The IRS uses a measure called <strong>Provisional Income</strong> (also called Combined Income):
  your adjusted gross income + non-taxable interest + 50% of your total Social Security benefit.
  The federal thresholds are fixed by law and do not adjust for inflation:
</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Single filers:</strong> If Provisional Income exceeds $25,000, up to 50% of your Social Security may be taxable. Above $34,000, up to 85% may be taxable.</li>
  <li><strong>Married filing jointly:</strong> Thresholds are $32,000 and $44,000.</li>
</ul>
<p>
  Because these thresholds never adjust for inflation, a COLA increase can push some beneficiaries
  across a threshold for the first time. We recommend using our
  <a href="/tools/retirement-tax-calculator/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Retirement Tax Estimator ↗</a>
  after any COLA increase to recalculate your tax exposure.
</p>
<p>
  To adjust federal withholding from your Social Security check, file
  <strong>Form W-4V</strong> (Voluntary Withholding Request) with the SSA. You can choose 7%, 10%,
  12%, or 22% withholding.
</p>

<h2>What About SSI, SSDI, and Other Programs?</h2>

<p>
  The 2027 COLA of 3.6% applied to all Social Security programs, including:
</p>
<ul>
  <li>
    <strong>Supplemental Security Income (SSI):</strong> The maximum federal SSI benefit rises to
    <strong>$1,029/month</strong> for an eligible individual and <strong>$1,545/month</strong>
    for an eligible couple. Because January 1, 2027 is a federal holiday, increased SSI payments
    begin on <strong>December 31, 2026</strong>.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI):</strong> SSDI benefits receive the same
    3.6% COLA increase as retirement and survivor benefits, effective January 2027.
  </li>
  <li>
    <strong>Veterans' benefits (VA):</strong> VA disability compensation and pension programs are
    adjusted by the same COLA rate as Social Security under a separate federal provision. The 3.6%
    rate applies to VA benefits beginning December 1, 2026 (paid January 1, 2027).
  </li>
</ul>
<p>
  The maximum taxable earnings base for workers also adjusted in 2027: wages up to <strong>$192,300</strong>
  are subject to Social Security payroll taxes, up from $184,500 in 2026 — an increase of $7,800.
</p>
